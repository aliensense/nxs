# Changelog

This file lists all notable changes to NXS. The changes cover the `nxs` tool and daemon, the `libnxs` runtime library, the NXS unit firmware, the personalities and images, and the hub directory. The format follows Keep a Changelog, and the versions follow the release tags.

## [Unreleased]

## [1.2.0] - 2026-10-19

The rig is a graph of self-described blocks: the api 3 declaration, one map at two doors, `nxs describe`, the Hub's rows and `libnxs` bring-up images.

### Added

- The unit's `DIAG` region names the error behind its counts. `VM_IO_ERRNO` holds the errno of the measure loop's latest absorbed I/O error, and `BUS_HEAL_ERRNO` that of the failed transfer before the latest bus heal. `nxs <unit> status` prints them beside the counts, and the C API reads any cell by index (`nxs_unit_read_diag_counter`). The Cyphal register `aliensense.nxs.io_errno` serves the first.
- `nxs ros2` logs every break in a unit's stream as it happens. A break is a sample its sequence numbers skip, or one the unit overwrote before the host read it. A device stamp that jumps over one sample is one too, and so is a synced stamp that moves against the device clock. The bridge counts them in one line when it ends. `stampstats.py` prints the stamps missing from a topic's cadence.
- `nxsd` runs one camera node per camera link that is up, from boot. On a host whose library has a capture-stack backend the node is the library's own. It publishes `mono8` frames and `camera_info`, stamped at the exposure from the sensor's start of frame. Elsewhere it is the GStreamer node the launch's `cameras:=true` runs, and the launch adds none beside them. `nxs <port> status` names a node that stopped with its last line, and a record of a daemon that is not running raises nothing. The nodes read a link's calibration from `/etc/aliensense/camera_info/<port>_<link>.yaml`.
- The global-shutter camera personality offers the all-pixel 2064x1552 scan at 10 bits as a fourth mode, `allpixel_2064x1552_raw10`, from the datasheet's register list. A pair behind the Hub runs it at full resolution when the declaration or `--mode` names it. A port with no declared mode keeps the mode it ran. The 12-bit full-resolution mode still runs one camera alone. The two share the geometry, so `--mode` names the depth, `2064x1552-raw12` or `2064x1552-raw10`, and a camera node's `mode` takes the mode's name.
- The IAM20680 personality accepts a 500 Hz sample rate.
- The camera map's driver reads the map's `STATE` before a `COMMIT` and holds the host I²C bus from that read until the unit reads READY. A mode switch, a stream gate or a trigger conversion holds it for the whole run. The map paints how long each kind of run took the last time, in `MODE_RUN_MS`, `GATE_RUN_MS` and `TRIGGER_RUN_MS`. The driver waits that long before its first `STATE` read. A sample drain or a follower's write therefore never starts against the unit's apply on the pod's bus. Interface Description §6.4 states both windows.
- `nxs_frame_copy` in `libnxs` reads a frame's luma plane out of the device buffer. On Jetson the frame session runs the capture stack's own stream in mailbox mode. A consumer that keeps up sees every frame, and a slow one skips frames instead of stalling the stack. A frame's `timestamp_ns` is the sensor's start of frame on `CLOCK_MONOTONIC`, placed to about a tick of the capture stack's counter.
- The global-shutter camera personality states the fast-trigger timing chart as a datasheet limit, `limits.fast_trigger`, and its 12-bit full-resolution mode its own wait registers. The LAWS record carries both. So a host reading the unit's trailer crops the triggered viewers of every mode, the 12-bit one included. An older tool reads the record's first fields and ignores the rest.
- A triggered link's capture record states its readout delay, from the exposure's end to the first valid line. It also states the sensor's exposure offset under a pulse-width exposure. `nxs_capture_timing_of` reads them beside `nxs_capture_pair_of`. So a program stamps a frame's exposure end at its start-of-frame stamp less the readout delay.
- A unit with no click node declares an empty socket. `nxs status` reports a unit that runs a personality its declaration does not name, `runs <personality>, which the declaration does not name`, and `switch` clears the slot naming what ran.
- The unit answers a second I²C address, 0x31, the camera door, beside the unit door at 0x30. It serves the cam personality's parameter rows at 16-bit register addresses, each with its name, unit and id. A host kernel driver maps its controls to the rows and commits the values in physical units, in one write. The map serves the running mode's ranges in those units, and a mode switch runs the personality's configure for the row, in standby. An apply runs the cam personality's `apply` action under the sensor's group hold, and a row it does not stage keeps its value. Its contract is the chapter Register map of the Interface Description.
- The camera map's layout holds 64 rows of 64 bytes and 16 modes, the capacity of a smart camera. A device serves its own count, and every cell past it reads 0. A pod serves at most 16 rows, the image format's parameter cap.
- A commit on the camera map is one write that ends on `MASK` and `COMMIT`. The staged cells run downward from `MASK`, so the write starts at the cell of the highest row staged. On the global-shutter camera a per-frame commit of the exposure and the gain, rows 0 and 2, is 23 bytes with the register pointer.
- A smart camera serves the camera door alone, at 0x31 on its port's own bus, with no unit door. `nxs generate` seeds it by its map and names the installed personality whose rows and mode table it serves, and `nxs <port> status` names it the same way. `on`, `off`, `set` and a capture's stream gate commit on its map, and `nxs switch` uploads it nothing. The declaration refuses one behind a Hub or beside a declared unit, naming the connector port. Its cam personality is of the `native` law family, `chip: native` with the map's physical limits in the facts file, no register and no bytecode. An experimental `imx428` is offered under `--experimental`.
- A pod's park, start or timing run that stages its action alone keeps the mode, the trigger and the timing the last run left. After `off` the camera map therefore reads what the sensor holds.
- After a configure or a timing run the unit applies the exposure and gain rows' values once, in the same run. The sensor then holds what the camera map reads, so a commit of one row never moves the other.
- A camera run whose slot takes another personality between its acceptance and its load ends with `ESTALE`, 133. A camera map commit ends the same way, and the unit reloads the map.
- A camera map's trigger row names its conversions by a registry: 0 free run, 1 fast, where the pulse width is the exposure, 2 sequential. A driver builds its trigger menu from the row alone.
- A cam personality's exposure row defaults to the declared default exposure, held under the default mode's shortest frame less the sensor's margin. A driver's exposure control therefore opens at a value the sensor takes.
- A cam personality's `temperature` row defaults to `TEMPERATURE_NONE`, the signed cell 0x80000000 the row reads until the head has read a temperature.
- A cam personality's rows carry `inck_hz`, the clock its head runs from, read-only and shown in megahertz. Its `mode` row names each mode, and a connector head whose image declares no mode row takes the one its facts list.
- A camera's rate row is `fps`, in frames per second. The camera map serves it as row id 2, `FRAME_RATE_MFPS`, in milli-frames per second. The declaration, `nxs tune`, `nxs <port> set fps`, `nxs <port> get fps` and the MCP's `suite_set` take it in frames per second. It offers the rates the camera's mode runs free at, and under the Hub's frame sync it is not declared.
- The camera map's exposure range follows the frame in effect. `MAX` is the frame less the sensor's margin, repainted by an apply that moves the frame period. A commit clamps the exposure under the frame it applies. A driver's exposure control therefore never offers a value the frame cannot hold.
- `nxs <unit> map check` proves a unit's map at both its doors over I²C, against the unit's own records. It reads the identity, the rows and the mode table, and runs the sensor once. It commits each row an apply takes inside the range in effect, and each range but the frame period past its maximum. It checks the refusals the contract names and the camera's values at both doors, and `--json` emits the `map_check` surface. On a smart camera's bus, where no unit answers, it proves the camera door against the installed personality whose rows it serves.
- Behind a pod and on a smart camera, `set` and `get` reach the head through its camera map. A knob is a row, by its name and in the row's unit. `exposure` also takes a time, `fps` a rate in frames per second, and `gain_db` the gain in decibels. `caps` lists the rows an apply takes, with `fps`, `gain_db` and `mode`, and `set` refuses a stream, trigger or read-only row. `get` reads the row's value in effect, never the sensor's registers, and `get mode` names the mode. Under frame sync `get fps` reads the Hub's frame-sync generator.
- A camera-map knob selects no window, so `set` on one link of a running pair keeps the other link's video. A pod's knob commits on its camera map on a connector port too. A value past a range row's bounds commits at the bound, and the line names the value in effect beside the one asked, `(60000 mdB asked, clamped to the row's maximum)`. A value off an enum row's list is refused, naming the values it takes.
- `nxs <port> <link> set mode <row>` switches a running link's row through the chain the bring-up walks, and `nxsd` follows the row each pod runs. `nxs <port> status` names a link that runs another row than it declares.
- A cam personality's `apply` action, action 4, writes the staged `frame_length`, `exposure` and `gain` run parameters under the sensor's group hold. They are in the sensor's lines and capture units. The unit converts the camera map's microseconds, milli-frames per second and millidecibels to them with the law the record carries. The `trigger` action, action 5, converts a running sensor to the staged trigger.
- The image format version is the product version's major and minor, 1.2 for this release. Every image carries the version of the wheel that built it, and firmware refuses an image of a later minor than its own with `NEEDS_NEWER_MINOR`. It refuses a format 1.0 image of 1.0.0 as another major's, since its header predates this one. Trailer record 13, `PARAMS`, carries one 64-byte row per parameter: a click personality's VM parameters, or a cam personality's public rows. A cam personality's record 12, `CAMERA_MAP`, carries the law the unit converts a commit with and the mode table. `nxs personality records` prints a personality's trailer record by record, the parameter rows one per line.
- `nxs-csi2`, the mainline V4L2 sub-device driver for the camera map, is public in the `nxs-drivers` repository. It offers each row of the map's mode table as a format and builds its controls from the parameter rows.
- The Jetson kernel driver `nxs-csi2-tegra` drives a pod's head through its camera map. Its gain, exposure and frame-rate controls commit the rows in their units, one write per frame at most. It reads the map's state before each commit, so a commit of the tool's is never lost.
- One kernel module, `nxs-csi2-core`, serves the camera map for both drivers, the mainline sub-device and the Jetson driver, byte for byte. Its constants come from the map's one source, the generated header `nxs-csi2-map.h`, which `scripts/vendor-driver-map.sh` writes into a driver repository.
- Both kernel drivers follow the apply contract. After a commit the driver keeps the bus and sends nothing for the time the map paints for the commit's kind. Then it reads `STATE` every millisecond for 20 ms, a NAK read as busy. A transaction the device does not acknowledge outside a window repeats after 1 ms, 8 times at most. Each driver checks the map's identity, its layout version and what the device is at probe, and its log line names them.
- Each kernel driver offers the camera map's rows the capture stack does not drive as V4L2 controls, with one id on every host. They are `Trigger Mode`, `Test Pattern`, `Black Level`, `Temperature`, and one control per custom row, named as the device names it.
- A camera node with the device tree property `aliensense,passive` is a camera whose stream reaches this host while another host owns the hub's control bus. The driver transfers nothing on that bus, takes its formats from the tree, and offers no row control.
- The Jetson kernel package, `nxs-jetson`, ships `universal_aliensense.ko`, `nxs-csi2-core.ko`, `nxs-csi2-tegra.ko` and `aliensense_generic_des.ko`. The camera map driver builds its frame-format table from the overlay's mode nodes, and a head without a unit keeps the universal driver.
- The camera map driver's four FRAMOS-named private controls are gone. `Trigger Mode` replaces `shutter_mode`, the map's rows give `Test Pattern` and `Black Level`, and `operation_mode`, which committed nothing, has no successor. The universal driver keeps its four.
- The unit's `DIAG` region serves the boot's reset cause at index 6, `RESET_CAUSE`, as the chip's flags, the watchdog and a brownout among them. So a unit that restarted under load is told apart from one that lost power. `DIAG_COUNTER_COUNT` is 16, up from 6, and a cell past the named counters reads 0.
- A personality declares up to 16 parameters, up from 8, and the C API's `nxs_report` carries 16 run values.
- The compiler refuses a name the unit would cut. A personality, parameter, output or port name takes 15 characters at most. A parameter's unit and an output field's unit take 6. The compiler also refuses a negative parameter value.
- Trailer record 14, `OUTPUTS`, carries a personality's output fields, one 64-byte row each, the rows the unit door serves. A field whose scale follows a parameter carries the scale at the parameter's compiled value. `nxs personality records` prints the record.
- Trailer record 15, `PORTS`, names the typed ports a personality's device offers, up to 16 rows of 32 bytes. So a host can name eight camera connectors with their buses. A click personality offers its board's output, `out`, and a cam personality its head's output, `out`. The Hub offers its links, its two CSI outputs and its control bus. A `PORTS` row carries no lanes, since a CSI port's lanes are a row of its block named for the port, `csi_a_lanes` for `csi.A`. `nxs personality records` prints the record.
- Trailer record 21, `PRESENTATION`, says how each parameter row reads: the unit a value shows in, a decimal exponent, and a name per value. A camera's gain row shows in decibels, its trigger row by the conversion's name and its stream row as `off` or `on`. A personality's facts file sets a row's `show`, `labels` and `id`, and `declare_param` takes `id`, `show=Show(unit, exponent)` and `labels`. A hub program's `declare_param(..., public=True)` makes a run parameter a row. `declare_row` declares what a declaration sets on a hub or host program, a row that no phase reads. `nxs personality records` prints the entries, `nxs get` shows a named value by its name, and `nxs set` takes the name.
- A click personality's last parameter is the `stream` row the compiler adds, 0 standby and 1 sampling. So `nxs caps` lists it after the declared ones, and a personality counts it among its 16.
- The IAM20680, IIM20670, FXOS8700 and MC6470 personalities declare an `orientation` row, the mount rotation of the sensor's frame, with the standard id 18, `MOUNT_ORIENTATION`. Its values are the 24 rotation codes, named `NONE` to `ROLL_270_YAW_270`. The unit composes the row onto its calibration, and `nxs set orientation` takes a code's name and refuses a personality without the row. The calibration record's orientation byte is reserved (record version 2), and the `aliensense.nxs.calibration.orientation` register is gone.
- The image format names the kinds MODULE, a compute module's personality, and GRAPH, a graph's compiled bring-up. `nxs upload` refuses either for a unit, whose store takes click and cam personalities.
- The image format names the kind HOST, 5: a host's description, its ports and its rows, with no program. The tool compiles it from the host plugin, and a unit refuses it. A Jetson offers each connector as a `cam<N>` CSI input and a `bus_cam<N>` I²C output. Its rows are `cam<N>_lanes`, the lanes it wires, and `cam<N>_driver`, naming who drives its Hub.
- A cam personality's `select()` dispatches on a range parameter as well as an enum, with one block for each value of the range. The values run from 0 to 126, as an enum's do. `>>` and `&` with an integer take a field out of a run value, so one parameter carries several packed values.
- `nxs <port> status` reads the Hub's two CSI outputs: each output's lanes, rate and controller reset, the output each pipe's stream goes to, and the PHY copy. A line per output sums them up, `port A: 4 lanes at 2500 Mbps, pipes Y and Z` and `port B: off`.
- `libnxs` runs a graph image, the bring-up of a declared camera port as one image. It is a released Hub program, sealed as it shipped, under trailer records 16 to 20 that carry the rig's facts and the walks. `nxs_graph_up` brings the port up by the library's policy, `nxs_graph_walk` runs one walk, and `nxs_graph_info` reads the records. `nxs_graph_set` sets one parameter on a link outside any walk. `nxs personality records` prints the records, and a unit's store refuses a graph image.
- `set` on a bare head runs its personality's apply on the host with the knob staged. That is the exposure, the rate as a frame length, or the gain. Behind the Hub both links' window opens first, so the other link of a running pair keeps its video. A knob the apply takes no parameter for is refused, naming the ones it takes. `--dry-run` names the apply, `cam0/B: would run <personality>'s apply on the host with gain 60`.
- A table-only cam personality whose class declares no `action` takes one from its stream gate. The compiler runs the class's configure as the configure, the gate's streaming value as the start and its standby value as the park. A class that stages the apply calls `stream_gate(True)` and `stream_gate(False)` in its start and park arms.
- `suite.yaml` declares the rig as a graph, `api: 3`, with its `nodes` by name and its `connections`. Each node names its `type` and its `parameters`, and the type sets what kind of node it is: host, hub, unit, camera, click or module. A parameter is a row of the node in the form `nxs describe` prints, or an identity pin: `address`, `serial` or `firmware`. A connection is keyed by the port that feeds, and a host offers each bus by its device's name, as `host.i2c-9`, `host.can0` or `host.ttyUSB0`. The one source of a Hub's `i2c` input owns the Hub's bus, and the node types derive what a declaration leaves out.
- A unit's click socket is a bus port that takes every click node wired to it, one per chip of the board on it. The file's order is the order of the unit's slots.
- `nxs generate` writes a declaration of the 1.0 form or of api 2 again in api 3. It keeps the earlier file beside it as `suite.yaml.1.0` or `suite.yaml.api2`. It refuses and names a value api 3 derives and states nowhere, such as a link's window. It names each unit whose socket the earlier file left unmanaged. A unit it finds on a CAN bus is seeded with the node id it answers at. Every other verb refuses an earlier declaration and names `nxs generate`.
- A pod an earlier declaration put at its own alias, as 1.1.0 did at 0x31 and 0x32, moves to its link's letter's alias. `nxs generate` names each pod it moves.
- `nxs generate` moves a frame-synced pair's rate from its cameras onto the Hub's `sync_fps`, so the converted declaration is in tune.
- Every declared value is judged against what its node offers at its place, and a refusal names the nearest values offered. A type nothing installed describes is refused when the declaration loads, with `nxs describe <type>` among the next commands.
- One law judges every connection, and an input takes one source. A port feeds a port of its type, and a head's output feeds a pod's head, a link or a connector. A connection whose two ports state different values for one attribute is refused, naming both rows. A Hub's `csi_a_lanes` must match its connector's lanes, so a Hub on a Jetson's two-lane `cam0` states `csi_a_lanes: 2`. A refusal that asks for other lanes names the host's row, as `the host's cam1_lanes: 2`.
- A unit's own rows are declared by name, such as `decimation`, `decim.temp` or `can_node_id`. `nxs switch` commits a unit's differing rows of one kind together through the unit's I²C door and persists them. It lists a row as set once the unit applied it, and a refused row fails the unit's line, naming the row and the reason. `nxs status` reports a differing row as `rows` drift. A unit on a CAN bus names its node id in `can_node_id`, and a click's mounting is its `orientation` row. A save of `nxs tune` keeps the rows no section shows, a CAN unit's node id among them.
- `nxs describe <type>` prints a block's description: its kind and product name, its ports with their types and directions, the identity pins it takes and its rows. Each row lists the values it admits in the form the declaration writes. A host plugin, a Hub, a unit module, `nxs` or `nxs-module`, a cam personality and a click personality each describe themselves. A unit's ports come from the contract. For a host it prints the bus forms the host offers, `i2c-<N>`, `can<N>` and `tty<name>`, with a serial port's `<port>_baud` row. `--json` carries the same document, the `describe` surface.
- The Hub's program declares the rows a declaration sets on it, and `nxs describe` lists them. They are `sync_source` and `sync_fps`, the `gain` and `exposure` locks, each link's supply in `link_a_power` and `link_b_power`, and `csi_a_lanes`, `csi_b_lanes` and `csi_b_copy`. Under frame sync the trigger's pulse sets the exposure, so `exposure` is not declared, and in free run `sync_fps` is not. The Hub's own rules narrow its rows in `nxs status`, `nxs tune` and `--schema`, on a host that reads the Hub from the release's assets too. Under frame sync the Hub offers no `sync_fps` where its cameras run at no trigger rate, as on an IMX335 port or a RAW12 pair. `nxs status` names the reason at the Hub's `sync_source`.
- A port's bring-up opens with the Hub's power phase, which drives each head's supply through the carrier's expander as `link_a_power` and `link_b_power` declare. It sets the expander's supply pins as outputs first. A training dip cuts both heads' supplies and restores the ones the declaration keeps on.
- Bare `nxs status --json` carries the declaration as its `graph`. Each node comes with what the rig answers for it, `present`, `absent` or `unknown`, and each connection with its two ports.
- A host wired into a Hub's stream and not into its control bus is passive. Its overlay gives each camera channel the camera door's driver marked `aliensense,passive`, with the mode table the camera door would serve. The driver then takes its formats from the tree and transfers nothing on the bus.
- `on` drives a Hub's `csi.B` that the declaration wires to a module. The port's image routes port B with the declared lanes, streams and copy.
- A passive node carries the camera door's modes in the device tree property `aliensense,modes`, six cells per mode. The cells are the width, the height, the bit depth, the pixel phase with 0xff for none, the lane rate in Mbps and the lanes. The first mode is the default, and a mode's value is its index.
- `nxs status` holds each Hub output to its lanes, with the streams the declaration sends over it. It judges a declared gain by each head's camera-map law, the law `on` composes with.

### Changed

- `nxs.h` and the `--json` documents keep their shape from 1.2.0 on. 1.1.0 previewed them, so a program built against its header rebuilds against 1.2.0.
- `nxs switch` restarts `nxsd` when the wheel was reinstalled under the same build, and redeploys a click panel whose compiled image changed under the same declaration.
- `nxs switch` flashes a pod that runs other firmware than it installs before the pod takes its personality. The port comes up after the flash, so the run never ends on a pod that refuses an image of the newer format.
- `nxs switch` waits for a port the daemon is still bringing up, however long its walk runs, up to 20 minutes. It ends the wait after 120 s only when the daemon brings no port up.
- `nxs <port> on` brings a port behind the Hub up from the port's graph image. The tool composes the image from the declaration, and `libnxs` walks it by its own policy. The policy constructs a cold port, trains a link that reads down and recovers once when video does not lock. Then it runs the declared frame sync and gain. `nxsd` brings its ports up through the same walk.
- `on` says each decision of the walk's policy in one line: `the port is cold: constructing it`, `a link reads down: training` or `video did not lock: recovering the links`. A link that locks at neither 6 nor 12 Gbps refuses the bring-up with `nothing answers on link B (no lock at 6 or 12 Gbps)`, and the construction prints no line per link.
- The walk uploads a pod's declared build when the pod holds another build or none, in one line, `pod B: <personality> uploaded to slot 1`. A pod's turn reads `pod B: configure (<personality>)`, without the slot.
- `off` behind the Hub runs the park walk of the port's image. It turns the trigger off, gates the capture output off and runs each head's park action. The host runs a bare head's park action from its personality, `pod A: park (the host runs the sensor image)`. A head that does not park reads `pod A: park (<personality>): failed, going on`, and the port still parks. A pod whose head never came up is not parked, `pod A: never came up, nothing to park`, so its unit reports no fault.
- A port with no Hub comes up, parks, gates its stream and takes a knob from its graph image, as behind the Hub. The image walks the head alone. A pod runs its actions, the host runs a bare head's, and a smart camera takes commits on its camera door. A head whose personality declares no `action` is refused before the bus, `link A's personality declares no action parameter, so no step starts or parks its head`.
- A declared gain reaches every camera head through the image's gain walk, a bare head by its personality's apply on the host. It is the Hub's `gain`, or on a connector port the camera's own `gain`. The gain is held to each head's law before the bus, `nodes.camera-cam1-a.parameters.gain: 300 is past <sensor>'s 255 dB`.
- `status` on a port that is up reads the link locks through the image's status walk. `stream`, `capture` and the delivery check switch the capture gate through the image's gate walks. `set sync` runs the image's sync and free-run walks, and the pair-line search its timing walk.
- A hub phase that faults names the walk's step, the register and the device, `cam0: up 7: hub 3 addon_or_arm: the hub's phase 3 ended VM_POLL_TIMEOUT at 0x0013 (device 0x6a, pc 412)`.
- One register map serves at both of the unit's I²C addresses. The unit door at 0x30 serves every cell and the camera door at 0x31 the capture cells. The map takes a 16-bit register pointer, has no selector register, and keeps nothing between two transactions. A command and its argument are one write, a session chunk carries its own byte offset, and the sample burst its cursor. The host interface is version 2, and a unit of version 1 upgrades through `nxs switch`.
- Every parameter has one of four kinds, APPLY, RUN, GATE or TRIGGER, for a click, a camera and a module alike. A click parameter that re-runs the configure is a RUN row, and one applied in place an APPLY row. Every personality that streams or samples carries a `stream` row of kind GATE.
- The device paints `APPLY_RUN_MS`, how long its last apply took. A host waits that long in silence after an APPLY commit, as it already did after a run. Then it polls `STATE` once a millisecond, a NAK read as busy.
- The device's own knobs are rows it owns, `fifo_depth`, `decimation`, `decim.<subject>` and `can_term`, committed like any parameter. `nxs set decimation.<subject>` names the subject in full, so `nxs set decimation.temperature 25` commits the `decim.temp` row.
- The I²C sample stream drains a unit's queue every 40 ms, up from 10 ms, or within half the time the queue holds if sooner. Fewer bursts start against the unit's own sensor reads on the pod's bus, and the default queue holds 340 ms at 250 Hz. A short `fifo-depth` at a high rate loses no records between drains.
- The unit's node id and CAN bit timing are device rows too, `can_node_id`, `can_bitrate` and `can_data_rate`, so the unit serves 13 device rows. A commit judges `can_bitrate` and `can_data_rate` as a pair and refuses one off the CAN profiles. `nxs commission` commits them, `nxs set can-bitrate` the rate pair, and `STORE_PERSIST` keeps them. The `CONFIG` record is gone. The subject-IDs are Cyphal registers alone, so `commission --subject` runs over the CAN or serial transport.
- `nxs commission` over I²C commits the node id, the terminator and the bit rates as one change, so a refused rate pair changes nothing.
- The unit keeps its calibration across the update to this release. The mount orientation is the `orientation` row of its IMU or magnetometer personality, which the declaration sets.
- `set_orientation()` of the Python client takes no `persist` argument. The orientation is the IMU personality's `orientation` row, kept by a save or set again by the declaration.
- The unit runs a pod's cam personality by commits of its rows alone. A configure is a RUN commit of the mode row, and a park or a start is a commit of the stream row. A trigger conversion commits the trigger row. `ABORT` stops the commit in flight within 50 ms, and the commit ends FAULT with `ECANCELED`.
- Under frame sync a pod's head converts by a commit of its camera map's trigger row. Beside a bare head it converts last, under the Hub's broadcast window, after every pipe re-arms. `nxsd` copies a pod pair's gain on the camera maps, in millidecibels. Per link it writes `camera: {sensor, mode, fps}` and `unit: {name, alias}`, per port `hub`, `sync: {source, fps}` and `camera: {gain_db}`. A mode is written by name. A value the laws default stays implicit, so a link declared with its sensor alone reads `camera: {sensor: '<vendor>,<sensor>'}`.
- The sweep of `nxs tune` walks each hub link by link through its windows, as `nxs generate` does. So a pod belongs to the link it rides, and two pods strapped alike on two links are two nodes.
- `nxs generate` seeds `suite.yaml` through the writer `nxs tune` saves with: the seed is what the panel saves for the same walk. It declares every unit it found, running a known personality or none. A pod is named `unit-<port>-<link>`, and a unit on a bus behind no link after its bus and address, `unit-i2c-cam0-30`. The seed names the hub bare, `hub: <vendor>,<deserializer>`, and names no bus where the host resolves the port's name. It declares no port where nothing answered.
- The MCP server gains an `upload` tool that compiles a personality and uploads it to a unit.
- `nxs <port> caps` marks the rates below a mode's guaranteed frame, `below 80.13 fps: beyond the datasheet's guaranteed frame (20.7.1)` for the 1920x1080 pair on 2 CSI lanes. A slower rate stretches the frame past the datasheet's recommended one. `--json` carries the rate as `fps_guaranteed`.
- `nxs <port> on`, `set sync` and `set fps` end on a delivery check. Every camera link they ran is counted for two seconds at the rate it runs, through the capture stack. A link that misses a frame, reports a capture-stack error or delivers more than 1 % off the rate is refused with the rates delivered, `cam0: 60 fps asked, 58.1 delivered on A, 57.9 on B`. The refusal names the highest lawful whole rate at or under the lowest (`nxs cam0 set sync fsync --fps 57`), or the lowest lawful rate when delivery fell under it. `on` then leaves the links unknown, and `set` puts back the sync or the rate that ran before. The check stops the tool's own viewers on those links first.
- `nxs <port> status` prints the last delivery check that passed under the sync line, `cam0: verified 30.0 fps 12 s ago (A 30.0, B 30.0)`, and `--json` carries it as `verified`. `nxsd` logs it after it brings a port up.
- `nxs <port> on` finds the line a pair behind the Hub needs on the rig: it reads the Hub's line-memory overflow probe around the delivery check. Where the probe reports an overflow, it retimes the pair 5 % longer and at that step refuses a rate the longer line no longer carries. The retiming runs from the deserializer datasheet's line up to twice it, and past that the command refuses with `cam0: the line memory overflows up to 1458 clocks` and names `csi_lanes: 4`. A count that falls short with the probe clear is refused at once with the delivered rates. The port record keeps the line found, and `set`, `caps` and `status` judge the port at it. `caps` prints `[pair line 10842 ns, found]`, `--json` names its `line_source` `found`, and `status` prints `line 805` beside the verified rate.
- The Hub's facts file states the deserializer datasheet's facts the laws use, each with its section. These are the CSI bandwidth factor of 1.16 (Table 7), the 32 kB line buffer of each video pipe and the minimum blanking. The minimum blanking is 40 pixels or 300 ns + 370 UI. A sensor mode states its datasheet register-list row with its section: the recommended frame V_TR, the line length and the rate at them.
- A frame-synced pair runs one gain, which the leading link's capture session decides. `nxsd` copies it to the other head every frame under the sensor's register hold, while the port runs frame sync with both links up. The journal reads `cam0: B follows A at 30 fps`, and `cam0: B stops following A (<reason>)` when the port runs free, parks, changes its rate or reloads.
- On a synced pair's links the viewers, `capture`, `nxs.cam.frames` and the ROS 2 camera node run with no digital gain, noise reduction or edge enhancement. Each runs with its own white balance, and the HUD names the link's part.
- `camera.gain_db` locks a synced pair's gain at one declared value, written once by `on`. Under free run it locks the pair together with `camera.exposure_us`.
- `nxs <port> status` names who decides a pair's gain under the sync line: `cam0 ae: A leads, B follows (nxsd copies the gain each frame: 21.4 dB on both)`, a declared lock with the exposure it runs, or each link's own loop. A followed pair whose gain `nxsd` does not copy prints `cam0/B: does not follow A (nxsd copies no gain for cam0)` with `sudo systemctl restart nxsd` under it and exits 1. `--json` carries the same as `ae`, and a bare `nxs status` names the gap under the port.
- `suite.yaml` may be written as JSON. It loads the same as YAML.
- Sample FIFO. The unit queues every sample and the host reads them in batches over I²C. A stream therefore loses nothing while the host is late by less than the queue. The default depth holds 85 IMU samples, 425 ms at 200 Hz.
- `nxs stream` over I²C drains the sample FIFO and reports lost samples.
- `nxs get fifo-depth` and `nxs set fifo-depth <N>` read and set the queue's depth, where 0 means all the storage holds. The Cyphal register is `aliensense.nxs.sample_fifo.depth`, and `nxs commission --save` persists the depth.
- `libnxs`, the runtime library, ships in each platform wheel with its C headers `nxs.h` and `Frames.h`. It holds the unit client over I²C, Cyphal/CAN and Cyphal/serial, the sample stream, time sync and the port bring-up. The `nxs` tool runs on it.
- Firmware: `CAM_RUN` runs a cam personality from a store slot on the pod's bus, and `CAM_ABORT` stops the run within 50 ms. `CAM_STATE` and `CAM_ERROR` report the run's state and errno.
- Firmware: a cursor written after the `SAMPLE_DATA` pointer reads a burst of queued samples. The device-parameter view (`DRIVER_SELECT = 4`) sets the FIFO depth over I²C.
- Firmware: `XFER_TYPE` 6 and 7 serve a cam personality's descriptor trailer, up to 2048 bytes.
- Firmware: the personality instructions `POLL_REG`, `PARAM_LOAD`, `PARAM_STORE`, `MUL_REG` and `DIVU_REG`.
- An offline bundle per Jetson Linux release, `nxs-<version>-bundle-<l4t>.tar.gz`, with the aarch64 wheel, the assets tarball and the kernel package.
- Each platform wheel carries the NXS Runtime Library License for `libnxs` as `nxs/_lib/LICENSE`. The Python code and the headers stay Apache-2.0.
- `NOTICE` lists the third-party parts of `libnxs`, the unit firmware and the bootloader with their licence texts. The wheel carries it, and the assets tarball carries it with `LICENSE-Apache-2.0` beside its own `LICENSE`.
- On a host that boots no camera bus, as a stock JetPack image does, `nxs switch` installs the camera kernel package's mux overlay. It puts the overlay under the generated boot label and asks for one reboot, with or without a declaration. A rig of pods alone needs the kernel package for this too. On such a host `nxs probe` and a declared port name `nxs switch` as the next step.
- The Interface Description states how `nxs.h` and the `--json` documents grow from 1.1.0 on. A released struct keeps its size and meaning, and a new field arrives as a new struct with its own function. A key added to a `--json` object comes with a new contract number.
- The pure wheel, `aliensense_nxs-<version>-py3-none-any.whl`, ships on PyPI beside the platform wheels. pip takes it on a host no platform wheel matches, so `pip install aliensense-nxs` gives a Mac the compiler and `nxs personality check` for the personality skills. It carries no runtime library.

### Changed

- The programs a unit runs have two names: a cam personality for a camera head and a click personality for a Click. They replace the words driver, descriptor and pair throughout the tool, the documents and the skills. The DSL classes are `ClickPersonality`, `RegisterClickPersonality` and `CamPersonality`. A click personality's facts file names it under `meta.personality`, and a facts file's `meta.kind` is `click` or `cam`. The shipped click personalities sit under `nxs/click_personalities/<name>/` in the wheel.
- A hub directory and the cam personalities replace the descriptor pack. A cam personality is its own node: `nxs personality install <directory>` copies it into the store, and every hub's links and the connector offer it. The hub ships as its own directory inside the wheel. `nxs personality install` takes no `--extends`, and `nxs --experimental` reads `$NXS_CAM_HUBS` and `$NXS_CAM_PERSONALITIES` in place of `$NXS_CAM_DESCRIPTORS`. `nxs <port> caps --json` names the port's hub under `hub`, `connector` for a port without one, in place of `pack`.
- The MCP tool table and the tools' descriptions name the declaration and read as sentences.
- A frame-synced port takes a whole rate of its mode's range that the trigger law and the lanes admit, judged as a free-running rate is. Every rate a refusal names is a whole rate the command accepts.
- A synced rate whose pulse period is shorter than the trigger frame is refused with the datasheet's prohibited period, `cam0: trigger frame <N> lines take <t> ms at this line, the next pulse lands inside them (datasheet 20.7.3)`. The refusal names the highest whole rate that fits.
- Every mode a cam personality's program carries runs without `--experimental`: `on`, `set sync fsync`, `caps`, `nxs tune` and `nxs tune --schema` offer it, and the `… is not shipped …` refusals are gone. The flag gates the hub and cam personality roots, the overlays and a development personality store alone. A port with no declared mode runs the largest mode its laws admit. That is 2064x1552 RAW12 for one global-shutter head on 2 CSI lanes, and 1920x1080 for the pair, whose program carries RAW10. `caps` marks a mode the unit's program does not carry `[no unit program]`.
- A pair in a RAW12 mode is refused naming the pair's program, `a pair runs the doubled RAW10 pixel path, so 2064x1552 RAW12 runs alone on the port`.
- A mode runs from 1.50 fps, the kernel driver's minimum, to the ceiling its datasheet frame and the lane law give at the port's line. No measured point narrows the range: one global-shutter head at 1920x1080 on 2 CSI lanes runs up to 160.49 fps. A pair runs the line the deserializer's datasheet gives its two packets, 729 clocks for the 1920x1080 pair on 2 CSI lanes. There it runs 1.50 to 80.13 fps free-running. Under frame sync it runs every whole rate from 2 to 80 fps, triggered at the mode's recommended frame. A refusal prints the range's ends inside it, the ceiling rounded down.
- `nxs <port> caps --json` names each mode's line source, `datasheet`, or `found` for a pair line `on` found on the rig. On a pair it lists the lowest and the highest whole rate frame sync runs each mode at.
- `nxs <port> caps` on a pair lists a range, a line and a sync pair for the modes both heads run together on the port's table alone. A mode the pair's program does not carry reads `[runs alone on the port]`, and a mode the table boots no row for reads `[no capture row]`. `--json` carries `runs_alone` and `capture_row`.
- A port that declares no rate runs 30 fps, inside its mode's range. The capture table's rows top out at the mode's own ceiling. That ceiling is the lower of its datasheet frame and one camera's lane ceiling on the port's CSI lanes. The rows default to the declared rate, 30 fps when none, never past that ceiling. A change of the declared rate moves the rows, and `nxs switch` installs the port's overlay and asks for a reboot.
- The capture table's rows come in a new order. The first `nxs switch` after an upgrade therefore installs each camera port's overlay again and asks for one reboot.
- The lane law counts the deserializer datasheet's minimum blanking on every line, the line that times a pair. One 1920x1080 RAW10 camera then fills 2 CSI lanes at 188.87 fps (4 at 346.11), and the pair's lane ceiling is 94.44 fps. The datasheet frame binds first for the shipped modes, so their ranges stay.
- `nxs <port> off` and `stream` name only the viewers they stopped.
- `nxs <port> set sync fsync` states the filler rows the viewers crop under the trigger, `viewers crop the trigger frame's filler rows (97 lines, datasheet 20.7.3)`, from the sensor's output-delay law.
- `nxs <port> on --mode` with a mode the port's capture table boots no row for is refused before any write, `cam0/A: 2064x1552 RAW12 boots on no row of this port's table (the table carries one pixel format, RAW10, chosen by the declaration)`. The refusal names the declaration that carries the mode and the modes the table carries.
- A command waits up to a second for another's hold on the bus. `stream` and `capture` hold it only while switching the capture gate.
- `camera_source:=argus` is refused on a synced pair's links.
- `nxs <port> set gain` and `set gain_db` on a pair whose links run one gain are refused. The refusal names who decides the gain and `camera.gain_db` as the way to set it.
- `nxs <port> off` and the park `nxsd` runs have each pod park its camera after the host's park sequence, `pod B: park (<personality>, slot 1)`. A parked unit's LED therefore stops beating for the camera. A pod the tool cannot park prints one line, such as `pod A: no park run (no answer)`, and the port still parks.
- An addressed `nxs status` over I²C names the cam personality the unit holds under the click personality's lines. It adds the slot and the last run: `Camera: <personality> (slot 1, last run DONE)`, `never run` before its first run, or `Camera: (none)` when the store holds none.
- Where `/boot/dtb` holds several kernel DTBs, as on JetPack 6.2.1, the boot table install takes the one that declares the booted module's compatible. When none or several match, the refusal names the files and `nxs switch --fdt <dtb>`. That command names the base DTB by hand, and is refused when the path names no file.
- On a camera host the declaration is `/etc/aliensense/suite.yaml`, which `nxsd` reads. `nxs generate` writes it there, and `nxs switch` creates the directory group-writable for the `i2c` group. A declaration left under `~/.config` is refused by `switch`, `status`, `probe` and `generate` with the move that fixes it. That move makes the directory first where it does not exist yet. `nxs tune` saves a declaration the user cannot write through the sudo helper.
- A save writes a port in the one spelling. A port's `camera: {mode: 'WxH@fps', sync: fsync}` is read as each link's mode, the rate and the sync, and never written. A file in that form takes the one spelling at its first save, with the backup beside it. A link's wiring keys, such as `ser`, `des_window` and `csi_vc`, are read and never written, since `hardware.yaml` records the wiring.

### Removed

- The host capture-budget warning of `nxs <port> set sync fsync`. A synced rate is judged by its mode's range and the laws of its sensors and the Hub.
- The shipped points. A mode carries no proven operating point, in the facts file, on the unit or in `caps`. The SHIPPED trailer record (type 9) is retired: no compiler writes it, every decoder skips it and its number stays reserved.
- The measured laws: the serializer's pixel tail, the Hub's pulse fraction, the lane-efficiency factor and the trigger fit. The readout resonance orders, the clean frame and the jump threshold, and the measured output delay are removed too. A law states a datasheet fact with its section, or arithmetic from such facts. The triggered viewers crop the datasheet's output delay, and the delivery check measures the rest on the rig.
- The store location `/opt/aliensense/drivers` of 1.0.0 is no longer read. A personality name resolves in `/opt/aliensense/personalities/`, then in the wheel's own.

## [1.0.0] - 2026-09-09

The NXS unit is a sensor computer, reached over I²C, Cyphal/CAN-FD and Cyphal/serial, that runs click personalities and takes firmware updates in the field. It shipped on the units, and 1.1.0 is the first public release of the tool.

### Added

- The NXS unit serves an I²C register map at 0x30, over a GMSL camera link or a direct bus. It is also a Cyphal node over CAN-FD, or over serial at 460800 baud.
- A Click on the unit's mikroBUS socket connects over I²C, SPI or UART. A personality may also sample the socket's AN pad and drive its PWM pad.
- A click personality is a Python class compiled to bytecode that the unit runs. `nxs upload` sends one at runtime with no firmware rebuild. The store keeps eight in flash, runs one at boot and moves to the next slot when a sensor does not answer its probe.
- Every output field describes itself, with its name, type, scale, offset, unit and semantic. A host decodes any sensor's samples in SI units with no sensor-specific code.
- Eight click personalities ship with the tool, for the IAM-20680, IIM-20670, FXOS8700, MC6470, MS5611, NEO-M9N, ZED-F9P and AS5047D.
- `nxs calibrate` guides a per-unit calibration of the accelerometer, gyroscope, magnetometer and encoder zero, with the mounting orientation. The unit keeps it and applies it to the SI outputs, and the raw stream carries the sensor's counts unchanged.
- A signed firmware update lands in the second of two slots, and the unit rolls back when the new image does not confirm itself. `nxs push-fw` sends one over any transport, and `nxs recover` arms the serial recovery.
- The `nxs` tool and its Python SDK run the same commands over I²C, Cyphal/serial and Cyphal/CAN. They probe, upload, stream, manage the store, commission node and subject IDs, calibrate, identify a unit and update its firmware.
- `suite.yaml` declares every unit with its link, its firmware pin and its click personalities. `nxs suite switch` converges the units to it, and `nxs suite status` reports what drifted.
- `nxs ros2` maps the self-described fields onto standard ROS 2 topics, with optional time-synced stamps and no configuration per sensor.
- `nxs mcp` serves the verbs to an AI agent, and a skill writes a click personality from a sensor's datasheet.
- `nxs cam` brings up a camera chain, a sensor behind a serializer and a deserializer link, from a directory that describes the hardware.
